# The catalogue flight-model recipe — one procedure, ten decks

**Subject:** the answer to [`module.md`](module.md) §Spec 4's central question, in full. **A catalogue
fighter flies on JSBSim, on a deck generated by ONE recipe from EIGHT published anchors by closed-form
inversion.** This file is the recipe: what it consumes, what it inverts, what it takes by declared
analogy, what it cannot produce, and the deviation band every deck must meet before its row may answer a
campaign question.

**Delimitation:** the class that loads these decks is [`module.md`](module.md); the per-row anchor values
are [`catalogue.md`](catalogue.md); the method this file generalises is
[`../mig29/flight-model-spec.md`](../mig29/flight-model-spec.md), and its `Documented` /
`Derivation path` / `Open` three-column discipline is kept.

**Status: BUILT 2026-07-28 as an EXECUTABLE recipe — `sim/tools/gen_air_decks.py` IS this file, and the
ten decks are its output.** `--check` regenerates into a temporary tree and diffs, so the committed
decks are provably what the recipe produces; a hand edit to a generated deck is a defect, not a delta.
**Six of the ten rows are `ACCEPTED` and four are `ALPHA`**: `## State` holds the residual table;
`## Gaps` holds §8; `## Knowledge` holds the
derivations that are worth having outside their step.

**Tags**, the campaigns' own: `[T1]` official · `[T3]` established specialist literature · `[T4]`
encyclopaedic · `[DERIVED]` computed from a named relation · `[ANALOGY]` taken from a *different*
aircraft's public data, declared as such · `[SET]` a FlightBox setting with a reason · `[TODO]` not
sourced and not set · `[DISPUTED]` sources conflict, both carried.

## Spec

### 0. The rule the whole recipe hangs on

[`../mig29/flight-model-spec.md`](../mig29/flight-model-spec.md) §5.4 step 4, restated because it is
this file's foundation and not a footnote:

> The envelope anchors constrain the **product** `T(M,h) / D(M,h)`, not `T` and `D` separately. Fitting
> the thrust deck and the drag polar against the same anchors is under-determined. **The rule adopted
> here: the thrust deck is fixed by the analogy plus the published statics and is then FROZEN; all
> residual error is absorbed by the drag polar.** If that later forces an implausible `CD0`, the
> *thrust* analogy is what is wrong — and that failure mode is only diagnosable because the rule was
> written down first.

That rule was written before the MiG-29 deck existed and it **worked**: two of the four anchors that
deck missed (`A3` ceiling +8.7 %, `A4` Ps −24.8 %) were traced to the thrust side, exactly as predicted.
The recipe inherits the rule unchanged, and inherits its diagnosis with it.

### 1. The eight anchors — and why they are these eight

The anchor set was chosen as the **intersection of what the recipe needs and what every deck row
publishes.** That intersection is not a happy accident: fighter data *is* envelope data, because that is
what fighters were sold and compared on.

| # | Anchor | Published as | Consumed by | Produces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Vmax at altitude (Mach + altitude) | `max speed kmh` + `max speed note` | §4 | `CD0` at high supersonic Mach |
| **A2** | Vmax at sea level (Mach) | the second line of the same field | §4 | `CD0` at low supersonic Mach, high `q` |
| **A3** | Service ceiling | `ceiling m` | §4 | **consistency probe** — the thrust lapse against the same polar |
| **A4** | Rate of climb at sea level, or `Ps` | `climb rate ms` | §4 | `CD0` subsonic |
| **A5** | g limit | `g limits` | §6 | the limiter — in `systems/FBFlightControl`, **never in the deck** |
| **A6** | Masses: empty · gross · max · internal fuel | four fields | §3 | `<mass_balance>` + tanks + the mass-closure probe |
| **A7** | Thrust: engine count, dry, afterburner | `eng1 number/kn/kn-ab` | §5 | the **frozen** half of the `T/D` product |
| **A8** | Geometry: wing area, span, length | three fields | §2 | the non-dimensionalisation everything else is written in |

**Two consistency probes come free**, because the sources publish derived quantities beside the
primaries: **`thrust/weight`** and **`wing loading`** must reproduce from A6 · A7 · A8. §Knowledge 1
evaluates both for all ten deck rows before a line of XML is written, and **five of the ten do not
close** — each miss localising to a mass the source did not name. That is worth more than any of the
numbers alone, and it is the same argument the MiG-29's four-way mass closure made.

contribution is to apply it N times instead of once.

### 4. The drag polar — the closed-form inversion

**The polar:** `CD = CD0(M) + k·CL²`, `k = 1/(π·AR·e)`.

**AND THE DECK HAS TO CARRY THAT SENTENCE LITERALLY, which it did not.** The induced term is written
against `aero/cl-squared` — the lift coefficient the deck actually produces — and only the leading-edge
suction loss stays a function of α. Two measured reasons, both defects of bookkeeping and neither of
level:

1. **A parabola sampled every 5° is read as a chord.** A supersonic dash sits at 1.5–2° of α. On `f15c`
   at M 1.93 the α-indexed table returned `CDi = 0.00519` against the polar's own `k·CL² = 0.00179`
   — **2.9×** — and the 0.0034 of invented drag was most of the 0.0045 by which the deck missed the very
   anchor it had been inverted at. Every α-indexed table in a generated deck now carries 1° knots
   through the linear range.
2. **`kCLmach` was double-booked.** At M 2 the compressibility factor takes 30 % off the lift, the
   aeroplane answers with 43 % more α, and an α-indexed drag table charges `(1/0.70)²` of the induced
   drag the inversion assumed. Indexed by `CL`, that cancels by construction.

#### 4.1 `e` — the one number the catalogue can derive from published data, once

`e` is not published for any aircraft. But **one row publishes both `CD0` and `(L/D)max`**: the F-5E
gives *Zero-lift drag coefficient CD0 = 0.0200* and *Lift-to-drag ratio 10.0* [T4]. For a parabolic
polar,

```
(L/D)max = 1 / (2·√(CD0·k))
⇒  k = 1 / (4 · CD0 · (L/D)max²) = 1 / (4 · 0.0200 · 100) = 0.125
⇒  e = 1 / (π · AR · k) = 1 / (π · 3.86 · 0.125) = 0.660
```

**`e = 0.66` `[DERIVED]`**, from the only row in the catalogue that publishes the pair, applied as the
recipe's default across the fighter rows. The honesty note travels with it: it is **one aircraft's
number generalised to nine others**, it is the recipe's least-constrained quantity, and it is
consequently the first term the attribution test's `band_deck` is swept over
([`module.md`](module.md) §Spec 11, ±10 % on `e`).

*Rejected:* Raymer's empirical swept-wing relation `e = 4.61(1−0.045·AR^0.68)(cos Λ_LE)^0.15 − 3.1`.
It is published and it works — evaluated for the MiG-21bis (`AR = 2.226`, `Λ_LE = 57°`) it gives
**0.782** — but **leading-edge sweep is not published for a single deck row** in the sources read, so
using it would mean inventing a sweep angle per row to feed a formula. A measured number from one real
aircraft beats a formula fed with sixteen guesses.

#### 4.2 `CD0(M)` — three inverted points

At `Vmax`, level flight: `T = D`, `L = W`.

```
q   = ½ρV²
CL  = W / (qS)
CD  = T / (qS)
CD0 = T/(qS) − k·(W/(qS))²
```

Evaluated at **A2** (sea level) and **A1** (altitude) this gives two points on `CD0(M)` in the
supersonic range. **A4** gives a third, subsonic:

```
Ps = (T − D)·V / W        ⇒   D = T − Ps·W/V        ⇒   CD0 = D/(qS) − k·CL²
```

**The transonic drag-rise SHAPE between them is `[ANALOGY]`** — taken from the pinned F-16 deck's
`*_M` correction structure — and only its **magnitude** is fitted to hit A1 and A2. The recipe cannot
do better: three points do not define a transonic rise, and pretending otherwise would be the failure
[`../mig29/flight-model-spec.md`](../mig29/flight-model-spec.md) §1.1 exists to prevent.

#### 4.2b The inversion reads the deck's TABLES, never the closed form behind them

`T(M,h)` and `CD0(M)` are continuous functions in this file and **sampled tables in the deck**, and
JSBSim interpolates the samples linearly. Where the sampled function is curved the chord is not the
curve: the turbojet ram surface is convex in Mach, so its chord sits **1.1 % below it at M 1.66 and
1.6 % at M 2.0**. An inversion against the closed form hands the deck a `CD0` for a thrust it does not
have — and at the anchor the thrust and drag curves are **tangent by construction**, so 1 % of thrust is
5 % of Vmax. Measured on `su7`: M 1.60 against an anchor of 1.74, with a polar that matched the deck's
own drag build-up to **0.5 %**. The rule is therefore absolute: **anything the recipe inverts, it
inverts against the number the aeroplane will read.**

#### 4.3 The ram-recovery factor, named beside every `CD0` it produces

`T` in the inversions above is thrust **at that Mach and altitude**, and it is the frozen half of the
product. It carries a ram-recovery factor that is `[SET]`, and — per the MiG-29 file's own instruction —
**every inverted `CD0` is published next to the ram factor it assumed**, so a later reader can re-solve
without re-deriving.

*Worked, on the validation row* (§Knowledge 2 carries the arithmetic): the F-5E's A1 anchor plus a
`[SET]` ram factor of 2.0 inverts to `CD0(M1.63) = 0.0351`, i.e. **1.75× its published subsonic
0.0200**. A transonic drag rise of ×1.5–2.5 is what a supersonic fighter has, so the recipe's inversion
and the catalogue's one published `CD0` are consistent — which is the closest thing to a validation this
method can have.

#### 4.4 A3, the ceiling, is a **probe** and not an inversion — and it is a *measured* one

**How it is measured is part of what it means.** A service ceiling is the altitude at which the STEADY
climb decays through 0.5 m/s, flown on the best schedule the aeroplane has, and every word of that has
had to be argued out of the instrument one at a time:

| Wrong | Right | What it cost |
|---|---|---|
| a constant-**Mach** schedule | constant **CAS**, swept over seven speeds | a MiG-25 cannot reach M 2.6 at 2 000 m, so a M 2.6 target held it level at its spawn altitude for the whole run and the harness reported **2 049 m** as a 20 700 m ceiling |
| the **highest altitude touched** | the altitude where the filtered climb decays | a fast schedule arrives with kinetic energy and **zooms past its own steady ceiling**: reading the peak measured **+28.9 %** on `su22` and turned the probe into a measurement of the entry speed |
| one **shared 400 kt entry** for all seven candidates | each candidate ENTERED ON ITS OWN SPEED, and none below the row's own 1 g stall | the slow candidates had to dump 220 kt through the vertical-speed demand's 300 m/s ceiling. `mig25` departed out of it and the harness booked the **5 293 m** it was diving through, at −165 m/s, as a service ceiling |
| **constant CAS only** | constant CAS **to a Mach crossover, constant Mach above it** — which is what a climb schedule IS | every constant-CAS line crosses the row's Vmax somewhere, and terminating there made the answer a function of the CAS GRID: `su7` **−14.0 %** and `mirf1` **−8.2 %** were the row's best schedule thrown away at the crossover. Under the crossover the fast candidates converge on ONE altitude per row (`su22` 18 188 m from 420, 480 and 540 kt alike) — which is the evidence that the number is the aeroplane's and no longer the grid's |
| the decay read on **vertical speed** | the decay read on **Ps = d/dt(h + V²/2g)**, the same energy definition A4 is written in | a climb can be BOUGHT from speed. `mig25`'s 540 kt candidate gained its last **889 m while its energy height FELL 7 400 m** — a decelerating zoom, still showing 0.6 m/s of climb |
| a **3 600 s** clock, reporting wherever it stopped | 12 000 s, and **a run that did not end on the criterion yields NO reading** | the truncated run was booked as a result: `mig23` **23 253 m** while still climbing at 7.8 m/s, `mig25` **15 963 m** at t = 3 600 s on a schedule that needs 8 166 s. That one rule alone is **−22.9 % → +2.8 %** on `mig25`, and the `Ps` criterion above trims the remaining zoom tail to **−0.5 %** |

**Eight of the ten rows now sit inside ±7.6 %, mean absolute residual 2.9 %** against 7.6 % before. The
two that remain are R14 and R19, and both are DECK statements, not instrument ones.

At the service ceiling `Ps → 0`, so `T(h_c) = D`. That constrains the thrust lapse against the polar
already fixed by A1/A2/A4. It is **deliberately not** used to solve for `e`: the service ceiling of a
supersonic fighter is not flown at best `L/D`, the published definition of "ceiling" varies between
sources, and the MiG-29's own ceiling missed by **+8.7 %** with a deck whose Vmax anchors were within
2 %. A quantity that noisy is a check, not a solver.

### 5. Propulsion — two analogies and one calibration row

`<turbine_engine>` needs `IdleThrust` / `MilThrust` / `AugThrust` as tables of (Mach, density altitude).
**No public thrust deck exists for a single engine in this catalogue.**

| Family | Rows | Analogy | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Afterburning turbofan** | `f15c` (F100-PW-220), `su27` (AL-31F) | the **pinned F-16 model's own F100-PW-229 deck** | the cleanest analogy the recipe has: the F-15C's engine is the **same engine one dash number away**, from a deck that is already in the tree and read-only. Scaled to the -220's published statics |
| **Afterburning turbojet** | `mig21` `mig23` `mig25` `mig17` `su7` `su22` `mirf1` `f5e` — **eight rows** | **none exists in the tree** | the recipe's one genuine construction job: **ONE turbojet reference surface**, built once and reused |

**The turbojet reference is calibrated on `mig21` and validated on `f5e`.** Why those two:

| Row | Why |
|---|---|
| `mig21` — **calibration** | the densest turbojet anchor set in the catalogue: two Vmax anchors (M2.05 at 13 km, M1.06 at sea level), a ceiling, a **hard climb anchor** (17 000 m in 8 min 30 s — a time, not a rate, so it integrates the whole thrust lapse instead of sampling one point), a takeoff run and a landing speed |
| `f5e` — **validation** | the only row in the catalogue publishing `CD0` and `(L/D)max`, so the inverted polar can be checked against a published one **inside the same engine family** |

**Clamp behaviour matters and is inherited:** `../f16/flight-model.md` §12.3 flags the pinned deck's
"thrust exactly 0 above 60 000 ft density altitude" as *"a wall, not a decay"*. **The reference surface
does not copy the zero column**; it decays, so every row's ceiling is approached asymptotically instead
of hitting a cliff.

**AND THE SAME WALL SITS AT THE OTHER END OF THE GRID, which is where it was actually found.** JSBSim
CLAMPS at a table edge rather than extrapolating (`FGTable::GetValue`, `Constrain(0, …, 1)`), so a
surface tabulated only **to** 70 000 ft is CONSTANT above 21 336 m: the aeroplane keeps its 70 000 ft
thrust for ever and has no ceiling at all. Measured, once §4.4's schedule was allowed to fly there:
`mig23` held M 2.40 in a **steady** climb to **30 044 m**, where the decaying law gives 3.2× less thrust
than its own polar needs. The density-altitude grid is therefore continued to **100 000 ft** — turbojet
rows by the same `σⁿ · ram` construction, turbofan rows by the ISA density ratio the borrowed 60k/70k
pair already is — so the tabulated law covers the whole envelope any anchor is flown in and the clamp
binds where nothing flies. **Nothing below 70 000 ft moved**: every inverted `CD0` reads the same
columns it read before, and the ten aero decks are byte-identical across the change.

**Spool dynamics** are `[SET]` per family and set **explicitly in both directions**, because
`../f16/flight-model.md` §12.3 records that the pinned deck's spool-down is 3× faster than its spool-up,
which is physically backwards.

### 6. Flight control and limits — outside the deck, on purpose

**Every catalogue deck is a RAW airframe**, like the MiG-29's and unlike the pinned F-16's: no FLCS
inside the XML. `systems/FBFlightControl` wraps it from the outside on the **`Manual`/raw path that
already exists and was already hardened** for the MiG-29 (`71cb99f`: the pitch-deflection cap and the
α-limiter both bind on that path).

| Quantity | Where | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| g limit (A5) | `FBFlightControl::GLimitG` | published per row. **The class had no such field until this round** and §6 had been promising one since the file was written: the A5 anchor was therefore measuring the AERODYNAMIC limit instead of the structural one (`f15c` 13.3 g against a published 9.0). 0 = the row publishes none, and then the α limiter alone binds — which is what F-16 and MiG-29 read, so both are bit-identical |
| α limit | `FBFlightControl`, `AlphaLimitDeg` | `[TODO]` for almost every row — **and its absence is a hard gate**: a deck with no α limit and no FLCS departs at full aft stick (measured on the MiG-29's own bare deck: a tumble to 180° of α). A row with no published α limit gets `[SET]` at the value its own measured `CLmax` implies, and the setting is logged |
| `PitchStickMax` | `FBFlightControl` | **`[DERIVED]`, §6.1** — it used to be `[SET]` at 0.85 on nine rows and nothing read it |
| Control power (`Cmde`) | the deck | **`[GEO]` from the row's own tail volume**, `-CLa_h·(S_h/S)·(l_h/c̄)·η`. It used to be **INV against A5** and that inversion is retired with its measurement, in §6.2 |
| Control power (`Clda`, `Cndr`) | the deck | `[GEO]`/`[SET]` from the same surfaces, unchanged |
| `core/FBFlightMonitor` | untouched | **it must not be taught anything about a catalogue row.** It is model-agnostic by construction, and a departure it calls is a defect of the deck — which is precisely what makes it useful here |

#### 6.1 The per-row gain preset — the thing this contract did not name

**`systems/FBFlightControl::Raw(PitchStickMax, AlphaLimitDeg, GLimitG)`.** Until this round every
catalogue row flew on `FBFlightControl::Mig29()` verbatim, and `air-bomber-intercept.fbm` measured what
that costs: **8 000 m of altitude in 242 s** at 1 g with the throttle at idle, ending in the ground. It
is the MiG-29's gain set with **two of the row's own numbers and one normalisation**:

| | |
|---|---|
| `AlphaLimitDeg` | the ROW's limit. The MiG-29's 26° on a MiG-25 (12°) parks the limiter 14° past the stall, where it limits nothing |
| `PitchStickMax` | **`[DERIVED]`: the cap at which full stick TRIMS 1.5× the row's own α limit**, `P = 1.5·\|Cmα\|·α_lim / (\|Cmδe\|·δe_max)`. Every row's limiter then owns the same 50 % reserve and no row can pull itself into a tumble on the cap alone — the one real hazard the MiG-29 round measured on a raw airframe. Ten rows: 0.127 … 0.278 |
| `GLimitG` | the published g limit (A5), 0 where none is published |
| the normalisation | the three g-branch gains scale with `P/0.60` (0.60 = the MiG-29's own measured cap). A g error then buys the same FRACTION of each row's authority, and since `P` is itself defined in multiples of the α limit, the closed loop is row-independent in the quantity it limits. Without it a row with three times the elevator power regulates with three times the loop gain |

**The same preset is what the anchor harness flies**, so the A3/A5/α anchors measure the aeroplane a
mission will actually fly and not a controller that exists only in the instrument.

#### 6.2 Why `Cmδe` is no longer inverted against A5

The old inversion set `Cmδe·δe_max = -Cmα·α_lim` — an elevator sized to **barely trim** the α limit at
full travel, i.e. an aeroplane with zero manoeuvre margin at its own limit. Two numbers killed it:

1. On `f5e` it returned **0.167/rad** where §2's own `[SET]` tail volume implies **1.254/rad**, a factor
   7.5. §2 and §6 were describing two different tails.
2. **Rotation.** The moment balance about the main gear at `Vr = 1.15·Vs` needs
   `|Cmδe| ≥ [d·CLmax/(1.3225·δe_max) + 0.432·kCLge·d] / c̄` = **0.415/rad** on `f5e` at §2's
   `0.15·c̄` main-gear offset. 0.167 could not lift a nose: full aft stick held from 167 kt to 226 kt at
   2.8° of pitch, and the take-off run measured **+130 %** on every row that publishes one, to within
   4 % — `f5e` 1 403 m against 610, `mig21` +128 %, `mig23` +126 %.

The `[GEO]` value satisfies both by construction and carries its own margin in the deck banner
(2.0×–3.0× the rotation demand). R12's remaining half is answered: the gear was not the residual cause,
the elevator was.

### 7. Build order, and the promotion gates

Each step is done when a named anchor is **measured** in `fb-gym` telemetry. No step starts before its
predecessor's anchor passes, or the later step's calibration absorbs the earlier step's error.

| Step | Build | Anchor | Failure diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `<metrics>` + `<mass_balance>` (§2, §3) | the **two free probes** (`T/W`, `W/S`) reproduce, and mass closure if the row has an empty mass | a probe that misses names the mass the source used (§Knowledge 1) |
| 2 | `<propulsion>` from the family analogy, statics from A7 | **A2**, then **A1**, on a first-cut polar | if A1 and A2 cannot both be hit, the **transonic rise shape** is wrong, not the statics |
| 3 | the polar: `e` from §4.1, `CD0(M)` inverted at three points | A1, A2, **A4** simultaneously; **A3** as the probe | ceiling reached but Vmax not ⇒ the thrust analogy is the suspect, per §0 |
| 4 | lift and high-lift devices | `CLmax` where a landing/stall speed exists; otherwise the row is **airborne-only** and says so | — |
| 5 | moments and control power | **A5**: full stick reaches the published g at the corner | insufficient authority ⇒ §2's tail volumes, which is the one place they matter |
| 6 | limits in `FBFlightControl` | A5 and the α limit | the limiter must be **overridable**, as the MiG-29's is |
| 7 | **measurement, not calibration**: corner speed, sustained and instantaneous turn rate, **peak roll rate and the roll plant `(a, K)`** | none — there is nothing to compare against for most rows | these become the row's own `FBPilot` hooks and are **declared accepted model properties**, exactly as the MiG-29's 241 °/s roll rate was |

### 2. The validation arithmetic, in full

The F-5E is the only catalogue row publishing a zero-lift drag coefficient. Running §4.2's inversion on
its A1 anchor and comparing against that published number is the closest thing the recipe has to a
validation, so it is written out rather than asserted.

```
A1:  M 1.63 at 36 000 ft (11 000 m)          [T4]
     a(11 km) = 295.1 m/s      ρ(11 km) = 0.3639 kg/m³      σ = 0.297
     V  = 1.63 × 295.1                      = 481.0 m/s
     q  = ½ · 0.3639 · 481.0²               = 42 097 Pa
     S  = 186 ft²                           = 17.28 m²
     qS = 42 097 · 17.28                    = 727 kN

     T(SL, static, AB, 2 × J85-GE-21) = 2 × 5 000 lbf   = 44.48 kN      [T4]
     ram factor at M 1.63                               = 2.0           [SET]
     T(M 1.63, 11 km) ≈ 44.48 · σ · 2.0                 = 26.4 kN

     W (gross, clean 15 745 lb)                         = 70.06 kN      [T4]
     CL = 70.06 / 727                                   = 0.0963
     CD = 26.4  / 727                                   = 0.0363
     k·CL² = 0.125 · 0.0963²                            = 0.00116

     CD0(M 1.63) = 0.0363 − 0.0012                      = 0.0351
```

Against the row's **published subsonic** `CD0 = 0.0200` [T4] that is a transonic rise of **×1.75**. A
supersonic fighter's rise is ×1.5–2.5, so the two numbers are consistent — the inversion did not produce
an absurdity, and it did so on a row whose `k` came from that same row's own published `(L/D)max`.

**What this does and does not prove.** It proves the inversion is dimensionally and physically sound and
that the assumed ram factor is not wildly wrong. It does **not** prove the recipe reproduces any row's
*envelope*, because that is what §7's steps and §7.1's bands are for, and none of them has been run.

### 3. Why a catalogue deck is a RAW airframe and that is the cheap side

The pinned F-16 deck carries a real FLCS **inside** the XML that FlightBox's own FBW has to override
(`fcs/fbw-override`). [`../mig29/flight-model-spec.md`](../mig29/flight-model-spec.md) §7 established
that this is **the exception in FlightBox, not the rule**, and that a conventionally-controlled airframe
needs no FBW reimplementation at all — `systems/FBFlightControl` writes `fcs/*-cmd-norm` and the deck
turns those into deflections.

Every catalogue row is therefore the *rule* case, and the recipe writes no flight-control system beyond
the plain surface channels. The cost is paid on the pilot side instead, once, and it has already been
