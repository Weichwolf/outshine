**Box is green on both counts, and it is the proof that a case costs no engine work**

*Box* -- tagged `core`, `testing` at the pin, published as `glTF`, `glTF-Binary`, `glTF-Draco`, `glTF-Embedded`.

**Criteria met and the picture within the bound, on the first run.** It was the first of the **118
models with no case** to get one, and it was chosen for being the smallest solid in the index rather
than the most interesting.

**What it establishes is worth more than the one count it moved.** A case was authored from the pin, the
framing rule in `src/gltf/Framing.h` and the manifest schema **alone** -- no engine change, no new
instrument, no reduction. The corpus went `criteria 27 met of 33` to `28 of 34` and `14 within` to
`15 within` in one dispatch.

**The framing rule was reproduced rather than quoted from another manifest**, which is what made it
cheap: world bounds `[-0.5, 0.5]` on every axis, radius `0.5*|(1,1,1)| = 0.8660254037844386` m,
`yfov = 2*atan(12/50)`, `distance = radius / sin(yfov/2) / 0.6`, eye at
`centre + (cos20*cos35, sin20, cos20*sin35) * distance`. The runner recomputes all of it and would have
refused a mismatch, so the numbers in the manifest are a quotation of a derivation and not a `[SET]`.

**One thing was checked rather than assumed.** The root node carries a 90-degree rotation about X, which
maps the cube onto itself -- so world bounds and accessor bounds agree here **by coincidence of this
subject**, and a camera derived from untransformed bounds would have been right for the wrong reason.
*Named because the next cube-shaped model will not be so forgiving.*
